The Real Cost of a Missed Call

Published by LLM Perfected · Last updated October 12, 2026

For home-services businesses, a ringing phone that nobody answers isn't a minor inconvenience — it's a direct, measurable revenue leak. Here's what the available data says, and what it looks like in dollar terms for a typical Bay Area trade business.

22%of inbound calls to small businesses go unanswered on average
37.8%of calls to home-service businesses specifically are missed, per call-tracking data
62%+of callers who hit voicemail will call a competitor instead of leaving a message

Why home services trades are especially exposed

Most HVAC, plumbing, and electrical techs are on a job — on a roof, under a sink, inside a panel — exactly when a new customer is calling. Average service call values for these trades commonly run from a few hundred dollars for a simple repair up to several thousand for an installation, so even a handful of missed calls per week compounds quickly.

Worked example: A shop fielding 60 calls a week that misses roughly 37% of them (≈22 missed calls) and converts about a third of answered calls into a booked job, at a blended average job value of $400, is leaving in the neighborhood of $2,900–$4,880 per week on the table in unconverted demand, before accounting for the lifetime value of a new customer relationship.

The Bay Area angle

With a median San Francisco home value well above $1.4M, the home-services jobs tied to that housing stock — larger HVAC systems, older plumbing, premium finish work — tend to skew toward higher ticket sizes than the national average, which raises the stakes on every missed call even further.
